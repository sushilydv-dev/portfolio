What makes it read as a real wolf

Four-beat walk

Paws land one at a time in a lateral sequence: left hind, left front, right hind, right front. Each paw is on the ground about 60% of the stride.

Diagonal trot

Wolves cover distance at a trot: diagonal pairs (left hind + right front) move together, with a brief moment of suspension.

Bounding gallop

At full speed the body flexes and extends: hind paws land almost together, then the front pair, with all four off the ground in between.

Legs that fold correctly

Front legs bend backward at the elbow, hind legs forward at the stifle, so sitting, lying and crouching fold the way a canine skeleton does.

Ears and tail talk

Ears go forward when focused, flatten when unsure; the tail rises and wags when happy, drops when stalking and streams out when running.

Cub behaviours

Play bow, mousing pounce, scratching with a hind leg, the full-body shake, a wobbly first howl and sleeping curled nose-to-tail.
